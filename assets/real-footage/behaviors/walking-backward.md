# Walking backward

> Untested against a live generator. The failure modes are predictions; replace them with
> what the first runs show.

## Camera

```text
[SUBJECT] walks toward the lens while the operator retreats backward down the same [PATH]. Each backward step adds an uneven vertical bob and a small roll. Near edges slide past faster than the background. [SUBJECT]'s size in frame stays roughly constant because both are moving. When [TRIGGER], the operator's step catches and the frame dips, then the operator corrects the framing once and keeps backing up until [END POINT], where the frame settles. The operator is on foot and backing up the whole time: the subject is not magnified by a lens from a fixed spot, and the motion is not a glide on wheels or rails.
```

## Fill-ins
- `[SUBJECT]`: who walks toward the lens.
- `[PATH]`: the aisle, hallway or sidewalk they share.
- `[TRIGGER]`: what breaks the rhythm (the subject speeds up, a cart rolls behind them).
- `[END POINT]`: where the retreat stops (the operator backs through a doorway, the
  subject stops at a counter).

## Notes
- Say the subject's size stays the same. That is what separates this from a zoom.
- Phone and documentary styles fit. A camcorder works if the clip is short.
