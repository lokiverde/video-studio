# Talk to lens

> One run so far (2026-10-29, guide section 16). The first five failure modes are
> predictions, read off the guards in the Ads with Cami prompt: no shouting, the same
> voice, no zoom, no music, no captions. On that run the eyes stayed on the lens, no
> caption appeared and the lean read as her body moving, not a zoom. The last two are what
> it showed: Seedance said the word an ACTION beat was tied to twice, and a 24-word line
> left the closing stare almost no time.

## Camera

```text
The phone is [HOW IT SITS], front camera on, filming [SPEAKER] talking straight into the lens. A faint constant wobble from [WHAT MOVES IT]. When [SPEAKER] leans in on [THE LINE THAT MATTERS], the face grows large in the frame and goes slightly soft at the close focus, then [SPEAKER] sits back and the frame settles where it started and holds. The eyes stay on the lens while speaking, with one quick glance down at [WHAT THEY GLANCE AT] and straight back. The framing changes only because the body moves: not a lens zoom, not a push-in and not an edit to a closer angle.
```

## Fill-ins
- `[HOW IT SITS]`: propped against the bathroom mirror at chest height, clipped in a dash
  mount, held at arm's length in one hand.
- `[SPEAKER]`: the one person talking, as named in CAST.
- `[WHAT MOVES IT]`: the hand holding it, the car idling, the counter bumped by an elbow.
- `[THE LINE THAT MATTERS]`: the beat of the story the lean lands on.
- `[WHAT THEY GLANCE AT]`: the prop, their lap, the door behind them.

## Notes
- The front-camera talking video people post every day. Pair it with the
  `phone-front-talk` style and the `talk-to-lens-clip` template, which carries the motion
  reference and the performance lines.
- The lean is the one move. Do not add a flinch or a whip pan to the same clip.
